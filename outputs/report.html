Islamic and conventional banks under the same shock: Malaysia, 2006-2026

Within-country local projections on Bank Negara Malaysia statistics. Working paper draft.
Generated: 2026-11-19 12:01

Summary

Question. When the same shock hits Malaysia, do Islamic banks transmit it differently from conventional banks? The comparison is within the country, month and loan purpose, so everything common to Malaysia cancels.

Credit quantities: no difference. Islamic and conventional financing for the same purpose respond alike to US monetary policy surprises, changes in the overnight policy rate (OPR) and global risk-off shocks. Both primary credit tests are far from significance (permutation p = 0.83 and 0.61) and the null holds in every robustness check.

Prices: Islamic banks reprice more slowly on both sides of the balance sheet. Measured against the OPR's own cumulative path, the average rate on outstanding Islamic financing passes through 0.83 at 12 months against 0.98 for conventional loans, while both banks' Base Rates pass through about 1.0. The Islamic 12-month deposit profit rate moves 0.28 on impact against 0.89 and catches up after about 18 months; Islamic savings rates pass through about half as much. The book-rate result is suggestive (permutation p = 0.015 and 0.037; Holm-adjusted 0.09 and 0.15) and is concentrated in the 2022-23 hiking cycle; the deposit-rate result is the strongest in the run but was not a pre-registered primary test.

Reading. Islamic banking in Malaysia smooths the price side of monetary transmission without changing the volume of credit. That is consistent with fixed-price sale contracts in the financing book and smoothed profit rates on the funding side, and it explains why a cross-country test on money-market rates and credit totals finds nothing.

Narrative

Search disclosure. The six primary tests and the Holm correction were fixed before estimation. Two revisions followed the first estimates. (1) A conditional placebo was added because the baseline conditions on lagged OPR changes and the unconditional placebo does not. (2) Pass-through is reported relative to the OPR's own path, because the raw responses implied that the Base Rate moves more than twice the OPR. Neither revision changes the primary tests. Intervals do not account for these revisions or for the many secondary horizons shown.

Limits. Only 15 OPR moves fall in the 2011-2026 rate sample, five of them in 2022-23; without that cycle the book-rate gap halves and its permutation p-value rises to about 0.2. The book-rate conditional placebo is borderline (t = -1.89). The SME block covers 2021-07 to 2026-09 only and finds implausibly large Islamic SME growth after hikes, most likely the sector's trend coinciding with the 2022-23 hikes; it is descriptive. Conventional series are derived by subtraction and inherit a 0.6% gap in the pre-2021 system totals.

Interpretation. Both price gaps point the same way: Islamic banks adjust what they charge and what they pay more slowly. On the asset side, sale-based contracts (murabaha, bai' bithaman ajil) fix the price at inception, so the outstanding book reprices only as contracts run off. On the funding side, profit rates are managed against reserves and investment-account returns follow realised asset returns. Quantities do not differ: borrowers and depositors face slightly different price paths, but the volume of credit in each purpose responds the same.
